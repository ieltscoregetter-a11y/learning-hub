Higher-Order Thinking Skills (HOTS)

Higher-order thinking involves analysing information, evaluating evidence, solving complex problems and developing original ideas.

Critical Thinking

Examine information and make reasoned judgements.

Creative Thinking

Develop original ideas and explore possibilities.

Problem Solving

Investigate challenges and develop appropriate solutions.

Metacognition

Plan, monitor and reflect on your own thinking and learning.

Bloom's Revised Taxonomy

Six cognitive processes help teachers plan learning objectives and design questions with different demands.

6. Create
Produce an original idea, product or solution.
Design, develop, construct, propose.
5. Evaluate
Make a judgement using evidence and criteria.
Justify, critique, recommend, assess.
4. Analyse
Examine parts, patterns and relationships.
Compare, investigate, differentiate.
3. Apply
Use knowledge in a particular situation.
Demonstrate, calculate, solve, use.
2. Understand
Explain meaning and demonstrate comprehension.
Summarise, describe, explain.
1. Remember
Recall facts, concepts and information.
Identify, define, list, recall.

All six processes have a place in effective learning. Prior knowledge supports more complex thinking.

Reference: Anderson, L. W. & Krathwohl, D. R. (2001). A Taxonomy for Learning, Teaching, and Assessing.

Question Transformation Lab

Explore how changing a question can deepen student thinking.

Science

Remember

What is evaporation?

Analyse

Compare evaporation at two different temperatures. Explain the relationship using scientific evidence.

English Literature

Remember

Who is the main character?

Evaluate

Was the character's decision justified? Support your judgement with evidence from the text.

Mathematics

Apply

Calculate the area of this rectangle.

Create

Design two rectangles with identical areas but different perimeters. Explain your approach.

History

Remember

When did the Industrial Revolution begin?

Evaluate

Assess the impact of industrialisation on workers' lives using historical evidence.

Useful question stems
  • What evidence supports your conclusion?
  • How are these ideas connected?
  • Which solution would you recommend and why?
  • What alternative approach could you develop?
